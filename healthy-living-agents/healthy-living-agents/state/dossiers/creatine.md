# Creatine monohydrate: dossier
**Claim:** Increases strength and lean mass gains from resistance training; possible small cognitive benefit under stress or sleep loss.
**Dose/form studied:** Monohydrate, 3–5 g/day maintenance (or 20 g/day ×5–7 days loading, optional).
**Best evidence:** Multiple meta-analyses of RCTs showing added strength and lean mass with training (e.g. Lanhers 2017, Sports Med; Chilibeck 2017, Open Access J Sports Med); ISSN position stand (Kreider 2017). Cognitive data smaller and mixed.
**Rubric:** Tier 3 | Effect 2 | Relevance 2 | Population 3 | Replication 3 | Bias 3 | Dose 3 | Safety 3 | Measurable 2 | Cost 2 | **Total 26/30**
**Verdict:** ADOPT
**Why, in two lines:** One of the best-evidenced, cheapest, safest supplements, but its benefit depends on resistance training, which Jamie doesn't currently do (Body change 2 fixes that).
Current 10–15 g/day is 2–3× the effective maintenance dose: no extra benefit, more cost and more GI upset. **Reduce to 5 g/day**, any time of day, with food or a drink.
**Interaction note:** raises serum creatinine. Tell the lab/GP; prefer eGFR from cystatin C if offered.
**Monthly cost (UK):** ~£3–5 at 5 g/day (Lidl).
**Re-review date:** 2027-10-15
